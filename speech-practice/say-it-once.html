      Five habits of being heard the first time

      You don't need all five at once. Practice one per day, in real conversations.

      • Point firstSay your main point in the first sentence. Details come after. Listeners hold on to the first thing they hear.
      • Volume 4On a scale of 1 to 5 where 3 is your normal, speak at 4. It will feel loud to you. To others it sounds normal.
      • Land the last wordKeep your breath and volume to the end of each sentence. Quiet speakers fade on the final words, and those often carry the meaning.
      • Pause, don't fillAt the end of each idea, close your mouth and pause for one beat. A pause sounds confident. "Um" doesn't.
      • One idea per sentenceIf you hear yourself say "and… and… and…", stop. Start a new sentence.
      • Open your mouthMumbling is often a closed jaw. Let your teeth part on every vowel. Crisp endings on t, d and k.

      Four shapes for any answer

      A shape gives your brain rails to run on. When you lose your place, you know which part comes next.

      PREP

      Point → Reason → Example → Point

      For opinions, suggestions, and "what do you think?"

      PointI think we should move the meeting to Monday. ReasonHalf the team is away on Friday. ExamplePriya and Sam both have leave booked. PointSo Monday gets everyone in the room.

      STAR

      Situation → Task → Action → Result

      For interview questions that start with "Tell me about a time…"

      SituationOur support queue had a two-week backlog. TaskI was asked to cut it in half. ActionI sorted tickets by type and wrote templates for the most common ones. ResultThe backlog dropped to three days within a month.

      What · So what · Now what

      Fact → Why it matters → Next step

      For status updates, bad news, and asking for help.

      WhatThe client approved the design. So whatThat means we can start building on Monday. Now whatI'll send the timeline to the team by Thursday.

      Headline + 3

      Headline → First → Second → Third

      For "tell me about yourself", summaries, and explaining how something works.

      HeadlineI'd be a strong fit for three reasons. FirstI've done this exact work for two years. SecondI learn new tools fast. ThirdI stay calm with difficult customers.

      When your mind jumps

      With ADHD, losing the thread mid-sentence is normal. The fix is a recovery line you've practiced, so you can steer back without panic. Say one of these, pause, and restate your point.

      • "Let me start with the main point."When you notice you began with background.
      • "The short version is…"When you've been talking too long.
      • "Let me back up. The key thing is…"When you went off on a tangent.
      • "To come back to your question…"When you forgot what was asked.
      • "Let me say that more simply."When you see a confused face.
      • Count on your fingers as you say "first, second, third". Your hand keeps your place when your head doesn't.
      • Write three keywords before a meeting or call. Only three.
      • End by repeating your headline. If you rambled, this saves the answer.
