00Summary

South Australia is building more homes than at any time in 40 years, but the industry is short of trades and supervisors, each home takes far longer than it did a decade ago, and builders are failing on thin margins. Demand is not the constraint. The constraint is how many jobs each supervisor, estimator and office worker can push through. That is where AI pays.

  • Record volume. 15,308 dwellings approved in the 12 months to July 2026 and 14,130 completed in the year to March 2026, above the government's 13,500-a-year target.[2] SA construction work done grew 11.5% in the year to June 2026, against 2.7% nationally.[4]
  • Capacity is the bottleneck. Adelaide's trade availability index was −0.79 in the June quarter 2026, among the worst capitals, and trade prices rose 5.1% in six months.[7] An SA home took 13.7 months from approval to completion in 2024-25, 74% longer than in 2014.[9]
  • Megaprojects will compete for the same people. The $8.5bn Osborne submarine yard started construction this month and could need about 4,000 workers at peak.[12][13] The $15.4bn T2D tunnels and the $3.2bn Women's and Children's Hospital run at the same time.[14][16]
  • Failure is visible and costly. Qattro left 200+ jobs worth $110m unfinished. Adelaide Designer Homes and JAC Homes followed.[27][28][29] Since 15 January 2026, penalties reach $550,000 for companies, and engaging an unlicensed subcontractor is now an offence.[31][32]
  • Builders already own AI. They haven't connected it. In Deloitte's 2026 survey of construction businesses in Australia and Asia-Pacific, 46% use AI tools, but only 16% have advanced digital capability and a quarter still run mainly on paper.[42] The gap is integration: AI connected to the systems builders already run (ClickHome, Databuild, Xero, spreadsheets) and to SA rules (the Planning and Design Code, CBS licensing, building indemnity insurance).
Recommendation

Be the local AI integration partner for Adelaide builders, and lead with one product that shows results in eight weeks

  1. Build Build Command, a demo over synthetic job data enriched with real SA planning zones and Bureau of Meteorology weather. Personalise it with each prospect's own published home designs and estates (§06).
  2. Sell a paid pilot of SiteVoice, a supervisor voice diary plus drafted homeowner updates, to a builder doing 100–400 starts a year. It uses data they already have, it shows results in weeks, and people approve every AI output before it goes out (§05).
  3. Offer Sub Check (subcontractor licence and insurance verification) as a cheap entry product. The January 2026 penalties make it an easy yes.
  4. Expand each account into Margin Watch (job cost and cash early warning) and Trade Flow (delay risk and trade booking). Then move into commercial builders with Tender Reader.

Scale of the prize: cutting two weeks from an average build across SA's 14,130 yearly completions frees capacity equal to roughly 470 extra homes a year with the same workforce (14,130 × 2 ÷ 59.6 weeks). This is arithmetic, not a forecast.

Costs, labour and productivity

  • Rider Levett Bucknall forecasts Adelaide tender prices to rise 5.1% in 2026.[10] Builders who signed fixed-price contracts at last year's rates absorb the gap.
  • Bricklaying, ceramic tiling, roofing and carpentry are the worst shortages nationally. Australia needs about 83,000 more skilled trades for housing.[7]
  • The Productivity Commission found housing construction labour productivity fell 12% over 30 years, while the whole economy rose 49%. The average residential building firm employs fewer than two people.[11]
  • Only 12% of SA's construction workforce are women (4% in trades), and the median age is 39.[6] Labour supply will not solve this quickly, so each existing worker has to get more done.

P1

SiteVoice — supervisor diary and homeowner updates

Lead pilot
Problem
Supervisors running 10–15 builds spend evenings on diaries, texts and calls. Homeowners ring because they can't see progress. Communication is the most common complaint in reviews.
What it does
  • Supervisor sends a voice note or photos from the car. SiteVoice writes the diary entry per job, logs defects and actions, and drafts messages to trades and suppliers.
  • Every week it drafts each homeowner's update from the schedule, the diary and photos, with a weather note. The supervisor or customer-care team approves each one with a tap.
  • A portal or SMS assistant answers "what's next on my build?" from that job's own data only. Anything about money, disputes or defects goes to a person.
  • A daily digest for the construction manager: what moved, what's stuck, who hasn't been updated in 10 days.
Data needed
Job list and schedule (ClickHome export or API, or a spreadsheet), supervisor voice notes and photos, and homeowner contact preferences.
AI underneath
Speech-to-text, an LLM that extracts structured facts into a fixed diary schema, retrieval over each job's history, and style rules taken from the builder's best past updates.
MVP scope
6–8 weeks to build. Pilot on 20–40 live jobs with 2–3 supervisors. Voice notes arrive through a simple web app or WhatsApp/SMS, so there's no new app to learn.
Price guide
$15k–$25k pilot. Then about $20–$35 per active job per month (≈$60k–$100k a year for a builder with 250 jobs in progress).
Proof metric
Supervisor admin hours per week, homeowner calls per job, update frequency, and review score before vs after.
P2

Sub Check — subcontractor licence and insurance compliance

Entry product
Problem
Since 15 January 2026, engaging an unlicensed subcontractor or misusing a licence number is an offence, with penalties up to $550,000 for companies on repeat offences.[31] Insurance certificates expire without anyone noticing.
What it does
  • Checks each subcontractor's licence against the CBS public register[68] and ABN status via ABN Lookup. Re-checks monthly.
  • Reads uploaded insurance certificates (public liability, workers comp) and SWMS with document AI. Extracts expiry dates and cover amounts, then chases renewals by SMS or email.
  • Blocks or flags a trade booking when a licence or policy has lapsed, and keeps an audit trail for CBS inquiries.
  • Optional: watches ASIC published notices for insolvency events among the builder's subcontractors and suppliers.
Data needed
The builder's subcontractor list with ABNs and licence numbers, plus certificate PDFs.
MVP scope
3–4 weeks. Easy to sell to any builder size, including self-serve for small builders.
Price guide
$99–$499 a month by number of subcontractors, or bundled with SiteVoice.
Proof metric
% of active subs verified; lapsed policies caught before work started.
P3

Margin Watch — job cost and cash early warning

Owner's product
Problem
Fixed-price contracts meet 5.1% trade price rises in six months.[7] Margin fade shows up at job close, too late. Variations go unsigned or unbilled, and stage claims go out days late. That cash squeeze is how Qattro failed.[27]
What it does
  • Joins estimate, purchase orders, supplier invoices and variations per job. Forecasts final margin every week and flags jobs drifting beyond a set tolerance, with the cost codes causing it.
  • Finds variations that were agreed on site (in diaries, emails or texts) but never signed or invoiced.
  • Watches stage completion and prompts the progress claim the same day. Tracks debtor days per stage.
  • 13-week cash forecast across all jobs. Plain-English weekly brief to the owner: "Three jobs in Riverlea are trending 4% under margin because brick costs rose; here is why."
Data needed
Accounting (Xero or MYOB), estimating and purchasing exports (e.g. Databuild), and the job schedule.
AI underneath
Invoice and PO extraction, cost-code matching, anomaly detection, a simple forecasting model, and LLM-written explanations with links to the source documents.
Price guide
$1.5k–$4k a month after a $10k–$20k setup.
Proof metric
Weeks between a margin issue appearing and being flagged; unbilled variations recovered; days from stage completion to invoice.
P4

Trade Flow — delay risk and trade booking

Speed product
Problem
With Adelaide short of bricklayers, tilers, roofers and carpenters, one missed booking can idle a job for a week or more. Supervisors re-plan by phone.
What it does
  • Predicts each job's next stage dates from the builder's own history plus Bureau of Meteorology rain forecasts, trade reliability and supplier lead times. Flags jobs at risk before the gap opens.
  • Confirms bookings with trades by SMS two days out, and offers re-sequencing when a trade drops out (e.g. bring forward a nearby job that's ready for that crew).
  • Groups nearby jobs by estate to cut supervisor and trade travel across the far north and south growth fronts.
Data needed
12–24 months of schedule history with actual stage dates, the trade list, and BOM forecasts and observations.
AI underneath
Gradient-boosted duration models, a rules layer for weather-sensitive stages (slab, frame, roof, brickwork), an optimiser for sequencing, and an LLM for the messages and explanations.
Price guide
$2k–$5k a month. A good fit for co-development with AIML's SME program.
Proof metric
Idle days between stages; average build weeks; % of bookings confirmed 48 hours out.
P5

Precon Desk — lot check, document chaser and selections

Adelaide-specific
Problem
Pre-site takes 8–16 weeks, and one late decision pushes site start further.[40] With PlanSA speeding up approvals,[35] the builder's own paperwork becomes the critical path.
What it does
  • Lot Check: from an address or lot, pulls the Planning and Design Code zone, subzone, overlays and Technical and Numeric Variations from open state data.[58] Summarises the rules that matter for the chosen design, flags likely issues (bushfire overlay, character area, minimum site area), and adds a site-cost risk note by region.
  • Document chaser: per job, tracks soil report, engineering, 7-star energy rating, BAL assessment, BII certificate and planning consent. Reads each document as it arrives to confirm it's the right one, and chases the consultant or homeowner.
  • Selections assistant: guides homeowners through colour and fixture selections in plain language, prices upgrades from the builder's schedule, and nudges before a delay costs site-start weeks.
Guardrail
Advisory only. Planning decisions stay with the relevant authority and building rules with the certifier.
Price guide
$1.5k–$4k a month plus setup.
Proof metric
Contract-to-site-start days; % of applications lodged complete the first time.
P6

Plan to Price — AI takeoff and quick estimate

Partner on the model
Problem
Estimators re-measure similar plans again and again, and custom jobs wait weeks for a price.
What it does
Reads a floor plan PDF, measures floor area, wall lengths, roof area and door and window counts, then maps quantities to the builder's rate library and inclusions to produce a draft estimate for an estimator to check. Many builder plans are vector PDFs, so dimensions and room labels can be read directly, which is more reliable than image recognition.
Build vs buy
Don't train a takeoff model from scratch. Use vector extraction, a vision LLM for raster plans, and integrate tools like Buildxact where the builder already uses them. Your value is the rate mapping, the SA site-cost rules and the workflow.
Price guide
Per plan ($20–$60) or $1k–$3k a month.
Proof metric
Estimator hours per quote; turnaround; variance between the AI draft and the final estimate.
P7

Tender Reader — commercial tender, RFI and claims copilot

Second market
Problem
Mid-tier commercial builders read thousands of pages per tender, write RFIs by hand and assess subcontractor claims against tight Security of Payment timelines.
What it does
  • Splits specs and drawings into scope by trade, finds gaps and conflicts, and drafts RFIs with page references.
  • Compares subcontractor quotes line by line (e.g. from EstimateOne), highlighting exclusions and qualifications.
  • Flags contract risks: liquidated damages, time bars, payment terms against SA's Security of Payment Act.
  • Drafts daily site diaries and delay notices from site records.
Buyers
Badge, Mossop, Kennett, Sarah Group, Leed, Exact Contracting and similar firms.[25]
Price guide
$1.5k–$5k a month per estimating team.
P8

Site Vision — photo QA and safety

Later / partner
What it does
Checks stage photos against a checklist (e.g. insulation present before plasterboard, sarking, tie-downs), and detects PPE and edge-protection issues from site photos.
Why later
Needs labelled photos from real sites and hardware in commercial settings. Start by collecting well-labelled stage photos through SiteVoice. That builds the dataset for free.

How to make it personal before the meeting

  • Load the prospect's public design names, sizes and "from" prices, and their display and estate locations, from their website. Use them only in their private demo, and don't republish their plans.
  • Put the synthetic jobs in the estates where they actually build, and use real rain history for those weeks, so the risk flags look like their year.
  • Summarise the themes in their public reviews (e.g. "communication after handover") to frame the problem. Show themes, not quotes, and be tactful.

Suggested stack: a React/Next.js front end, a Python FastAPI backend, Postgres with PostGIS and pgvector, MapLibre for the lot map, and a frontier LLM API such as Anthropic's Claude for extraction, drafting and answers. Three-week build plan for the demo

Week 1
Download P&D Code zones, subzones, overlays and variations. Load BOM rainfall. Write the synthetic data generator. Build the portfolio board with status chips and a job page timeline.
Week 2
AI summaries per job ("why is it late?"), homeowner update drafts, and a chat that answers from the job's data and cites the diary entries it used. Margin Watch view with fade and unbilled variations.
Week 3
Voice note → diary flow (upload an audio file). Lot Check map. Plan to Price on 3–5 vector plans. A personalisation script that loads a prospect's designs and estates. Rehearse the 20-minute script.

Running cost for demos is small: hosting in the tens of dollars a month and LLM usage in the cents-to-dollars range per demo. The expensive part is your time.

The 20-minute meeting

0–3 min
Their world in numbers: their estates, their designs, the Adelaide trade shortage, build times, and January's penalty changes. Ask how many jobs each supervisor carries.
3–8 min
Portfolio board. "These six jobs are where you'd lose weeks this month, and here's why."
8–13 min
Play a supervisor voice note, show the diary, the trade SMS and the homeowner update. Approve one live.
13–17 min
Lot Check and Plan to Price on one of their own published designs.
17–20 min
The offer: a paid 2-week AI Opportunity Audit, or straight to an 8-week SiteVoice pilot on 20–40 jobs, with agreed KPIs measured before and after. Ask for a schedule export, sample homeowner emails and two supervisors to pilot with.

Channels that work in a small market

  • Industry associations: Master Builders SA and HIA SA events, breakfasts and awards nights. Adelaide is small; one good case study travels fast.
  • Software partners: ClickHome, Buildxact and estimating-software resellers. You add AI on top of their product rather than competing with it.
  • AIML Industrial AI SME Grant Program: gives SA SMEs access to AIML's machine learning engineers (support in kind, not cash).[61] Co-apply with a builder client to develop Trade Flow's forecasting.

Objections you will hear

They sayYou answer
"We already have ClickHome."Good. It stays the system of record. We read from it and write drafts and tasks back. Nobody learns a new system.
"AI makes things up."It only answers from your job data and shows its source. Nothing reaches a homeowner or trade without a person approving it.
"Our supervisors won't use another app."They send a voice note or photo the way they already text. That's the whole interface.
"Where does our data go?"Australian hosting, isolated per builder, never used to train models, full audit log, and deletion on exit.
"It's too expensive."The pilot has agreed KPIs. If the numbers don't move, you don't continue.

Funding to extend your runway

  • R&D Tax Incentive: a refundable offset (currently 43.5% for eligible companies under $20m turnover) for genuine experimental work such as the forecasting and plan-reading models. Get an adviser to confirm eligibility.
  • Industry Growth Program: early-stage commercialisation grants of $50k–$250k for SMEs in National Reconstruction Fund priority areas.[65] Check fit before applying.
  • SA Seed-Start: state grants for innovative early-stage SA startups commercialising a product.[64]
  • SA's new $50m Research and Development Productivity Fund (2026 budget; details still emerging).[63]
  • AIML Industrial AI SME program: engineering support in kind.[61]

10Legal, privacy and delivery risks

RiskWhat appliesWhat to do
ScrapingWebsite terms, copyright in plans and drawings, and personal information on public registersPrefer open data (CC BY). Use a builder's plans only in their own private demo. Summarise review themes rather than copying text. Don't build homeowner lead lists from the DA register.
PrivacyPrivacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles. The small-business exemption does not cover businesses that trade in personal information. Automated decision-making transparency duties start 10 December 2026.[67] A statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy also now applies.Privacy policy and data processing terms from day one. Keep a person making any decision about an individual. Minimise and delete homeowner data you don't need.
AI regulationThe National AI Plan (2 Dec 2025) dropped mandatory guardrails in favour of existing laws. The new AI Safety Institute advises but can't compel.[66]No AI-specific licence is needed, but consumer law, WHS and privacy still apply. Avoid overstating what the product does in your marketing.
Professional liabilityEstimates, code checks and compliance flags can be wrongLabel outputs as drafts. Licensed people sign off. Never certify building or planning compliance. Carry professional indemnity insurance. Cap liability in contracts.
AccuracyLLMs can invent factsAnswer only from retrieved job data and show sources. Keep an evaluation set per product. Log every output with its approval.
Customer failureBuilder insolvencies are running at record levels[30]Bill monthly in advance. Avoid depending on one client. Check the client's financial signals before large custom builds.
AdoptionSupervisors are busy and scepticalUse voice and SMS instead of new apps. Choose pilot champions. Report hours saved back to the supervisors themselves.
